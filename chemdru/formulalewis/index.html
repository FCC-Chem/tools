How to use this tool

  1. Type element letters directly (e.g. CO). Click a Sub button to insert an atom-count subscript right where your cursor is (e.g. click "3" after "CO" to get CO₃).
  2. If it's an ion, click a Charge button (e.g. "2−") — this always replaces any previous charge and sits as a proper superscript.
  3. Press Build. Bare atom symbols appear on the canvas; drag any atom to rearrange the layout.
  4. Pick Single, Double, or Triple, then click one atom and then another to draw that bond. Using a bond tool on a pair that's already bonded raises the bond order by one step (single \u2192 double \u2192 triple) — so clicking Single twice on the same pair turns it into a double bond.
  5. Pick Lone pair and click an atom to place a lone pair on it (up to four). Lone pairs automatically settle into the open space around the atom, away from its bonds, and reposition themselves if you add more bonds or drag atoms around.
  6. Click any bond line or lone pair directly to remove it and return its electrons to the budget.
  7. Once you think you're done, press Check octet — atoms without a full outer shell (8 electrons, or 2 for hydrogen) will highlight red.
  8. The bar tracks electrons placed vs. total available — you can't place more than the formula allows — and the log on the right records every move.
