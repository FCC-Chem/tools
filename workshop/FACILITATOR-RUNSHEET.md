# Facilitator run sheet — 90 minutes

For you, not the participants.

---

## The night before

- [ ] Both example tools load at `fcc-chem.github.io/tools/`
- [ ] Actions tab shows a green run
- [ ] Every invited person has **accepted** — check
      `github.com/orgs/FCC-Chem/people`. Pending invites are the #1 day-of
      time sink.
- [ ] Handout printed. Paper, not a link. People lose tabs.
- [ ] Your demo tool decided and **rehearsed once** — know the prompt cold
- [ ] `_template` open in a tab, ready to hand to anyone who stalls
- [ ] Room wifi tested on the actual guest network they'll use

---

## Timing

| Time | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | **Live build.** You, on the projector, prompt to live URL. | No slides. Talk while you type. Let a mistake happen and fix it out loud — that's the most valuable 30 seconds of the day. |
| 0:15–0:20 | Everyone opens the repo, finds `_template`, copies it | Walk the room. Catch anyone not signed in *now*. |
| 0:20–0:25 | Paste the starter prompt, describe their tool | **Enforce small scope.** Push back on anything involving files or email. |
| 0:25–1:05 | Build time | You float. See triage below. |
| 1:05–1:15 | Commit both files, watch Actions run | Everyone hits the catalog page together. This is the payoff moment — make it feel like one. |
| 1:15–1:25 | Round robin — open each other's tools | Everyone says one sentence about what they made. |
| 1:25–1:30 | Post one idea in Discussions | Ends the session with a next action, not a goodbye. |

---

## The 15-minute live build — say these things out loud

- "I'm not going to write any code. Watch what I actually do."
- "I'm being specific about constraints. That's the whole skill."
- "It got that wrong. Watch — I'm telling it what the right answer is,
  not fixing it myself."
- "Now it's live. That URL goes in my Canvas page."

Pick something you can genuinely do in 12 minutes. A significant-figures
practice generator or a unit converter is plenty. Resist showing off.

---

## Floor triage during build time

**The perfectionist** — still refining at 0:50.
> "Ship what you have. Commit it as `wip`. You can improve it next week."

**The over-scoper** — trying to parse a Canvas export.
> "That's a phase-two tool. Today, build the calculator it would need
> inside it."

**The stalled** — blank prompt box at 0:30.
> Sit down next to them. Ask what they explain most often in class. Type
> their answer into the prompt for them.

**The broken-output** — page renders blank.
> Don't debug. Have them paste the whole file back to the AI with
> "this renders blank, find the error." Model the behavior you want.

**The one who didn't do pre-work.**
> Pair them with someone who did. They watch. They set up their account
> after. Do not stop the room.

---

## Hard rule

**Everyone leaves with a live URL.** That is the success metric — not
comprehension, not code quality, not whether the tool is any good.

At 1:00, anyone without something working gets the `_template` copied
under their name and shipped. A trivial live tool beats an ambitious
unfinished one, because they'll come back for the second one.

---

## What to say about safety (2 minutes, during the live build)

Don't make it a lecture. Say it once, plainly:

> "This repo is public. Never put student names, IDs, or grades in it.
> Never put a password or API key in it — those get scraped by bots
> within minutes of being pushed. And nothing with exam content, because
> public repos get permanently mirrored and deleting doesn't undo it.
> Everything we build today runs in the browser and stores nothing, which
> is why this is safe."

## After the workshop, same day

- [ ] Post in Discussions: everyone's tool linked, one line each
- [ ] Email the catalog link to the whole department, including people
      who didn't attend
- [ ] Put the next show-and-tell on the calendar **now**, before the
      energy fades

## Why these things die

Momentum, not difficulty. A repo with six tools and no activity for two
months reads as abandoned and nobody adds a seventh.

Cheapest fix: 30 minutes a month, and you personally add one tool between
sessions.
