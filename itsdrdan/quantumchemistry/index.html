Electron mass

me = 9.109 × 10−31 kg

KE = ½mev²
v = √(2KE/me)

Use kinetic energy in joules and mass in kilograms to obtain speed in m/s. This is the nonrelativistic model.
🌈 Light & energy

c = λν   |   Ephoton = hν = hc/λ
c = 2.998 × 108 m/s
h = 6.626 × 10−34 J·s
1 nm = 10−9 m
1 MHz = 106 Hz
1 eV = 1.602 × 10−19 J
NA = 6.022 × 1023 mol−1

In a vacuum, shorter wavelength means higher frequency and photon energy. Intensity relates to the number of photons, not energy per photon.

🪜 Bohr & matter waves

For one-electron species: En = −(2.179 × 10−18 J)Z²/n²; hydrogen has Z = 1. Bohr radius: rn = a₀n²/Z with a₀ = 0.529 Å. ΔEatom = Efinal − Einitial. Upward transitions absorb energy; downward transitions emit a photon with energy |ΔE|.

de Broglie: λ = h/(mv). Convert mass to kg. Heisenberg: ΔxΔp ≥ h/(4π); Δp = mΔv when applicable.

Each orbital holds at most 2 electrons; a shell contains n² orbitals and up to 2n² electrons.

📦 Filling orbitals

Aufbau: fill lower-energy available subshells. Pauli: two electrons per orbital with opposite spins. Hund: put one parallel-spin electron in each degenerate orbital before pairing.

Order: 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p 5s … For transition-metal cations, remove electrons from the highest principal shell first: 4s before 3d. Neutral Cr and Cu are common exceptions.

📈 Periodic trends

Atomic size generally increases down a group and decreases across a period. First ionization energy generally rises across and up, with exceptions. Cations are smaller than their neutral atoms; anions are larger. More shielding reduces effective nuclear attraction.

🔦 Photoelectric calculations

Photon energy: E = hc/λ. Maximum kinetic energy: KEmax = hν − φ (emission requires hν ≥ φ). Threshold: ν₀ = φ/h. Electron speed: v = √(2KEmax/me). Use me = 9.109 × 10−31 kg.

⭕ Radial & angular nodes

For hydrogen-like orbitals: radial nodes = n − ℓ − 1; angular nodes = ℓ; total nodes = n − 1. A radial node is a spherical surface; angular nodes include planes or cones depending on the orbital.

Probability density & radial distribution

ψ is a wavefunction amplitude. |ψ|² is probability density per unit volume and is nonnegative. For a normalized angular wavefunction, P(r) = r²|R(r)|²; P(r)dr gives the probability in a thin spherical shell. For hydrogen 1s, P(r) peaks at a₀, while |ψ|² peaks at the nucleus. Nodes occur where the relevant wavefunction is zero.
