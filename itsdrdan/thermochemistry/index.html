    Hess’s law: equation matching
    1. Compare each given equation with the target.
    2. Reverse an equation when needed and reverse the sign of ΔH.
    3. Multiply or divide every coefficient and ΔH by the same factor.
    4. Add the equations. Cancel equal amounts of identical species on opposite sides.
    5. Add adjusted enthalpies. Check that the remaining equation is the target.

    Enthalpy stoichiometry

    Balance first. The stated ΔH belongs to the whole equation, not automatically to one mole.

    grams ÷ molar mass → moles
    q = n × ΔH / ν (ν = balanced coefficient of the specified species)

    For a missing formation value, substitute all known coefficient-weighted terms into products − reactants, then solve for the unknown.

    Choose the right equation
    Temperature changes: q = mcΔT
    ΔT = Tf − Ti
    Phase changes: q = nΔH
    Mass to moles: n = m / molar mass
    Multiple stages: qtotal = q₁ + q₂ + …

    Use q = mcΔT on a slope. Use q = nΔH on a plateau at a transition temperature. A pure substance changes phase at constant temperature at a fixed pressure.

    Endothermic: q > 0
    Exothermic: q < 0

    Phase-change recipes
    ChangeNameHeat
    Solid → liquidMeltingAbsorbed
    Liquid → solidFreezingReleased
    Liquid → gasVaporizationAbsorbed
    Gas → liquidCondensationReleased
    Solid → gasSublimationAbsorbed
    Gas → solidDepositionReleased

    Boiling is vaporization at the boiling point. Evaporation also changes liquid to gas, but can occur below the boiling point.

    Reading diagrams and curves
    • Phase diagram: vertical axis is pressure; horizontal axis is temperature.
    • A boundary separates two phases that can coexist in equilibrium.
    • The triple point is where solid, liquid, and gas coexist in equilibrium.
    • A heating-curve slope means temperature is changing within a phase.
    • A plateau means phase change; energy changes intermolecular potential energy rather than temperature.
    • Plateau heat = end heat coordinate − start heat coordinate.

    The phase diagram here is schematic, for a typical substance with a positive solid–liquid boundary slope. It is not water’s phase diagram.

    Units and rearrangements
    1 kJ = 1000 J
    1 cal = 4.184 J
    1 kcal = 1 food Cal = 1000 cal
    m = q / (cΔT)
    c = q / (mΔT)
    Tf = Ti + q/(mc)
    K = °C + 273.15
    °F = 1.8(°C) + 32

    A temperature difference of 1 °C equals a difference of 1 K. Use °C throughout this game.
